Applies to subcontract heat treatment of AX-4CR13-080 at Härterei Weser GmbH (supplier V200). Set points are demo values for this fictional plant.

Process steps: load axles in baskets with spacers, harden in a protective atmosphere, quench in oil, wash, temper in furnace TO-3, air cool, then hardness check on the first and last basket of every load.

Tempering is the step that converts brittle as-quenched martensite into tempered martensite with usable toughness. If the real tempering temperature is lower than the set point, hardness stays high and toughness drops. Typical signs are HV10 above the upper limit, low bending force and flat, bright fracture surfaces with cleavage facets. A correctly tempered axle that is overloaded shows dimples on the fracture surface (ductile fracture).

Furnace control: the tempering furnace thermocouple must be checked against a reference thermocouple every 3 months (system accuracy test). Any deviation over 5 °C must be corrected before the next load. Temperature charts are kept per load and linked to the heat-treat batch number on the delivery note.

Common causes of out-of-spec hardness after tempering: thermocouple drift, wrong program selected, overloaded basket (parts in the middle do not reach temperature), shortened soak time, a basket skipped in tempering.

Sampling: when a supplier mixes several baskets in one delivery batch, the goods receipt sample must include parts from different baskets. Five parts from one basket do not represent the batch.
